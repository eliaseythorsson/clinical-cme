# Curriculum in themed blocks with a capped drill-down

Learners were pulled from one unfamiliar topic to the next ever deeper, with no coherence. Rounds now run in blocks (default 12 rounds, counted in rounds, not calendar time) themed on one domain: about 60% of short questions from the theme, 25% spaced repeats due from any domain, 15% wildcard, and the case always on the theme. The next theme is proposed automatically (weakest or longest-unvisited domain) and the learner can override it in one line. Drill-down happens only within the theme and at most two consecutive rounds per sub-topic; an off-theme miss gets one targeted retest after a few days, then normal spacing. A missed topic waits at least ~3 days, and a gap closes only when an item tests that gap.

## Consequences

The theme is shown to the learner ("Block: gastroenterology, round 4 of 12"). This deliberately gives up domain-level blinding for coherence; topics stay blind. Existing ledgers keep all history, gaps and due dates; the first block starts at the next round.

## Blocks as built (issue #16)

- The ledger holds the block (`block`: theme, rounds recorded, length) and the finished ones (`blocks`). Planning only reads; a block is written when its first round is recorded. So a ledger from before blocks needs no migration step: it has no block, the next round proposes one, and nothing else is touched. Every recorded round counts in the block, including a one-off round on another domain the learner asked for.
- The 60/25/15 shares are met over the block, not in each round. Rounded per round, the default three short questions would be 2/1/0 every time and the wildcard would never appear; instead round n gets what n rounds are owed less what was given. Some rounds therefore have no repeat slot, and an off-theme repeat can wait one round longer than its date.
- A share with nothing to give passes its slot to the theme (no repeats due on a new ledger; no untested topic left outside the theme). Repeat slots take what waits outside the theme first, because the theme's own due topics already have the theme slots. A large backlog of due topics outside the theme was therefore worked off at about one per round: coherence was chosen over clearing the backlog fast. Issue #79 changed that (below).
- Within the theme, what is due comes before new topics, with one slot kept for a new topic when the round has none; when every topic of the theme has been tested, the one tested longest ago is revisited on another decision point. The case avoids the topic of the previous round's case. The cap on drilling one sub-topic is issue #18.
- The proposed theme is deterministic: weight for new material (blueprint weight, corrected self-rating, emphasis), raised for open gaps, damped for four blocks after a domain's own block; domains with fewer than four blueprint topics are not proposed (three topics cannot fill twelve rounds) and reach the learner as repeats and wildcards. Importance counts as well as weakness: a weak low-weight domain can lose to a middling high-weight one. Issue #80 changed both halves (below).
- A round prepared ahead is used only for the round of the block it was planned for. A round prepared by a version without blocks is set aside once.
- The learner changing the theme (issue #17) builds on `propose_theme` (theme, one-line reason, ranking) and `start_block` (a chosen theme, filing the block it replaces).

## When a prepared round is kept (issues #41, #42)

- A settings change that cannot alter a round (testing mode, reminders, helpers, the overnight task, showing the coding, the review interval, the weekly target) keeps the prepared round; every other setting, including one the engine does not know, sets it aside and the command says which. The list is short on purpose: a wrongly kept round is worse than one written again.
- The ledger's `gen` counts every write, so a prepared round is tied to `content_gen`, the generation of the last change that could alter a round, kept in the ledger only while it differs from `gen`. Rejected: restamping the prepared round at each neutral change (it is not always in the store copy that was read, and it would be one more file to publish) and a fingerprint of the ledger's content (every new ledger field would have to be sorted into it).
- When the mix in force differs from the mix a prepared round was written for, the learner's latest choice wins: the prepared round is set aside and `start` says which mix is used and why. Rejected: playing the prepared round and applying the mix a round later, which shows the learner a round they had just switched away from.
- A page mix written against an older ledger (`base` stale) is applied when its time is later than the time the mix was last set; this compares the page's clock with the engine's, which the first design avoided. A clock that is wrong can let a page setting override a chat change made shortly after it; the opening line says which mix is used, so it does not pass unnoticed.

## Changing the theme as built (issue #17)

- The proposal is said, not stored. The last round of a block prints the next theme with its reason in one line (`next_block.line`, from `save` and `close`); the ledger is only written if the learner changes it. A learner who says nothing gets the proposed block, as before.
- A change always starts a new block of 12 at round 0. Mid-block, the block left behind ends where it is and is filed with the rounds it had. The alternative, relabelling the running block and keeping its count, was rejected: "round 7 of 12" on a theme with one round in it would be untrue, and the shares are owed over a block of one theme.
- A theme left mid-block counts as having had its block, so it is damped in the next proposals like any other. The learner turned it down; proposing it again at once would undo that. Its tested topics still come back as due repeats.
- "Pick another" walks down the same deterministic ranking. The themes turned down are kept in the pending block (`declined`) until its first round, so asking twice does not alternate between two domains.
- The learner's choice beats the size rule: a domain with fewer than four topics is never proposed but may be chosen, with a warning. The planner already revisits a theme's topics on other decision points when it runs out. Issue #80 removed the size rule (below).
- One command, `theme`, separate from `settings`: it changes the block, not a setting, and it has to print the line to say. It places the ledger the way `settings --out-dir` does, which also empties the prepared round (it was written for the old theme).
- The change is made between rounds. A round in progress is finished on the theme it was planned on; the engine cannot see a round in progress from the ledger, so this is a rule for the examiner (`state_schema.md` §4.1), not a check.
- The page shows the theme and a bar for the rounds done. It does not show the proposal for the next block: computing it there would copy the ranking into the page, and the close message and the next opening line already say it.

## Spacing rules as built (issue #15)

- The three-day minimum applies to every tested topic, not only to misses: the calibration rules (overconfident, underconfident) could otherwise undercut it. It is a floor on the interval, and the planner also refuses a topic tested in the last two days, so ledgers that already hold a next-day due date need no migration.
- Gaps have ids. The plan names the gap an item should retest, the item in the round names the gap it did retest, and only such an item, scoring 4 or more, closes it. A gap not yet retested gets one targeted retest once it is three days old; after a failed retest it follows its topic's spacing.
- The correction of comfort ratings is applied when domain weights are computed, not when the rating is stored: the rating that counts is the lower of the stored rating and the mean mastery of the domain's tested topics, rounded down. This also corrects ratings already inflated in existing ledgers and leaves the learner's own rating intact for the progress review. Ratings for untested domains (from setup) count as given: there is nothing yet to check them against.

## Drill-down limit as built (issue #18)

- "Sub-topic" is the blueprint topic, the unit that is scheduled. The limit has a hard part and a soft part. Hard, in the planner: a topic that was in both of the last two recorded rounds is not planned for the next one, whatever its due date, gap or reason (new, due, retest, revisit); only the learner's one-off `--allow-repeat` overrides it. It counts rounds, not days, so it holds at any cadence; the price is that after a long break a topic from the last two rounds waits one round more.
- Soft, in the schedule: once a topic's drill-down is used up, a further miss sets its interval to at least seven days instead of three. In the theme that is a miss in the round straight after a round on the same topic; outside the theme it is any miss on a topic that already had an open gap or was missed the time before. So outside the theme: miss, one retest after the rest, then never sooner than a week until the domain has its block; inside the theme a topic can be drilled in two rounds in a row, then pauses. Without the soft part the hard cap alone would give two rounds on, one off, for as long as the topic is missed.
- At two or three rounds a week the three-day rest already keeps a topic out of the next round, so in the theme a missed topic could return every other round for the rest of the block. That was within the issue's wording (two in a row) and was left as it was; issue #79 changed it (below).
- "Missed" means not answered well by the bar that closes a gap (4 or more, not overconfident, no confident error). Good answers keep their spacing.
- A gap written again after a failed retest used to count as new and got another targeted retest three days later, which outside the theme was the old pattern through another door. It now keeps `last_retested`.
- No migration: ledgers holding short due dates from before get the limit from the next round on. The example ledger `drilled` holds the old pattern (seven rounds, every repeat missed, three theme topics in all seven) and the tests show the next rounds capped.
- When a theme has nothing left that is allowed, the plan names only the domain and the author picks the topic; the limit cannot reach that choice. It did not occur in the tested cadences (a round every 3, 4, 7 or 10 days).
- With a change of theme (issue #17): a round is in or outside the theme by the block it is recorded in, so the new theme's topics are drilled as theme topics from the next round and the old theme's count as outside it. A wait given outside the theme ends when the topic's domain becomes the theme: the planner brings back an open gap of the theme whose topic was last tested under another theme. A pause given inside the theme stands.
- The 12-round test of "pick another" showed that the theme of the block just finished could be proposed again once a new block was chosen but not begun (the finished theme was then no longer "the last"). `propose_theme` now also leaves out the theme of the block before a block not yet begun.

## Backlog and the pause in the theme (issue #79)

Decided with the learner on 2026-11-26 (issue #37, points 1 and 2); points 3 (a seven-day pause), 4 and 6 stay as built.

- **A large backlog gets more room.** While six or more topics are overdue, repeats take 40% of the short questions and the theme 45%; the wildcard keeps 15%. The learner's condition was balance: the theme keeps the largest share. "Overdue" is the count `status` gives as `due_count` (date come, whatever the domain), so the number the learner can see is the number that decides. It is read afresh at each plan: below six the next round is back at 60/25/15.
- How the shares switch inside a block: the 60/25/15 mix is worked out as before, and in a backlog round the theme hands the repeats the round's part of the 15-point difference (what the first n rounds are owed, rounded up, less the first n−1). Rejected: running the block's largest-remainder sum on 45/40/15 in backlog rounds and 60/25/15 in others. The two sums disagree on the wildcard, so a block whose backlog came and went could end with 4 to 10 wildcards instead of 7 (four short questions). The chosen way never moves a wildcard slot, and a block spent wholly in a backlog lands within a slot of 45/40/15. Rounding up gives the first backlog round its extra repeat at once.
- The plan's `block.backlog` says the backlog shares were used and why (`overdue`, `shares`). The learner is not told: the opening line stays the theme and the position.
- **The pause in the theme.** A theme topic missed on its second round of the current block (in a row or not), or any later one, waits at least `DRILLDOWN_PAUSE_DAYS` (7), as a topic outside the theme does after its one retest. Before, only a miss in the round straight after another round on it paused it, so at two or three rounds a week a missed theme topic came back every other round. The first miss in a block still comes back after the three-day rest: drilling once stays. A good answer is not paused (it keeps its spacing), the same as outside the theme. The rounds counted are those recorded in the block, read from each session's `block`, so a round applied again (`save` run again, `override`) counts where it was recorded, and a new block, even on the same theme, starts its count afresh.
- The planner holds the pause too: a theme topic missed on its second or later round of the block is not planned before its date, by a gap awaiting its retest or by a revisit when the theme runs out, as with the learner's stop (issue #75). Without that, the gap's one targeted retest would bring it back three days after it was written, which is the pattern the pause removes. Only `--allow-repeat` overrides it.
- With the pause, a theme can more often have nothing allowed to give. A short question's theme slot then names the domain alone only while the theme has an untested topic left for the author (not one already taken by the round); otherwise the slot passes to whatever the ledger has, because an author told "an untested topic" with none left could only pick a topic the limits hold back. The case stays on the theme.
- Example ledgers: the plans of `mid_curriculum`, `overdue`, `stale_prepared` and `unreflected` (9 to 13 overdue) trade one theme slot for a second repeat; `drilled` (11 overdue) keeps its mix in its round 8 and only reports the backlog; `fresh` is unchanged.

## The theme proposal: measured weakness, any area (issue #80)

Decided with the learner on 2026-11-26 (issue #37, point 5, and the objection that the number of topics in an area is arbitrary).

- **Measured weakness.** Results reached the proposal only through open gaps and the self-rating, so a heavier, stronger area beat a lighter, weaker one. The score now also multiplies by a factor read off the mean current mastery of the area's tested topics: 1.5 at 2.5 or below, 1.0 at 3.5, 0.7 at 4.5 or above, linear between (two segments, so 3.5 is exactly neutral); nothing tested counts 1.0. It is applied in the proposal only, not to the weight for new material (`domain_weights`), which already corrects the self-rating by the same mean and steers wildcards and new topics. Weight still leads: Pulmonary (1.0, mean 3.5) stays ahead of Endocrinology (0.6, mean 2.5), 1.0 against 0.9; against Pulmonary at 4.5 Endocrinology wins, 0.9 against 0.7. The one-line reason already names the mean; the override is unchanged.
- **Any area can be a theme.** `MIN_THEME_TOPICS` is gone. An area with fewer than four blueprint topics gets a short block of two rounds per topic (`block_length`: three topics, six rounds; two, four), whether proposed or chosen. Two rounds per topic matches the pause in the theme (issue #79): a topic is drilled at most twice in a block before it waits, so a longer block on a small area would mostly revisit paused topics. The shares are met over the short block the same way (round n gets what n rounds are owed). A block keeps the length it started with, so a block written before this change keeps its 12.
- **One code per area.** The default blueprint listed "Emergencies / Toxicology" twice, as `TOX` (three topics) and `EMRG` (two). They are one area, `TOX`, with five topics; the topic ids stay, so `EMRG-01` is a topic of `TOX` and no history moves. The markdown blueprint already had one heading; the parser now makes a heading one area, coded by its first topic, instead of one area per id prefix. Existing ledgers are migrated when read (`migrate_domains`, with `migrate_profile` in `migrate_ledger`): areas with the same name merge into the first, every code in the ledger follows, topic records take their area from the blueprint. Rejected: a fixed `EMRG → TOX` alias table, which would not cover a learner's own blueprint with the same duplication. A self-rating given for both keeps the lower (asking for more practice costs nothing, as for the overconfidence correction); emphasis keeps the higher. The old code still names the area wherever the learner or the examiner can type one (`theme --set`, `--domain`, a review's ratings), and a topic in the blueprint is always filed under the blueprint's area, whatever an item's `domain` says. Migration does not count as a write (`gen` is unchanged); the ledger is stored merged with the next command that writes it. A round prepared ahead on the theme `EMRG` is set aside once, as for any theme that no longer matches.
- Example ledgers (their `ledger.json` files keep `EMRG`, as old ledgers do; `examples.load_ledger` reads them as the engine does): `mid_curriculum` now proposes Endocrinology (mean 2.0, an open gap) over Pulmonary (mean 5.0), `unreflected` General Medicine (mean 3.0, a gap) over Pulmonary (5.0), `stale_prepared` Gastroenterology (mean 1.0, a gap) over Pulmonary (5.0); their plans, schedules and applied rounds follow from the new theme. `overdue` keeps its plan; its TOX self-rating becomes 2 (the EMRG rating, lower than TOX's 4.0), so the reflection's comfort leaves it at 2.0 instead of moving it to 3.3. `fresh` and `drilled` are unchanged.
